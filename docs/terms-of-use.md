## What Trainr does

You answer some questions about yourself and your training, and Trainr writes you
a weekly workout plan. You log your sets against it, and the next week is written
from what you actually lifted.

The plans are written by the app itself, on your device, not by a person.
Everything you answer and log stays on your device.

## Training is your risk to take

**Trainr is not medical advice, and the plans are not written by a doctor or a
trainer.**

Before you start any new training, check with a doctor — especially if you have
an injury, a condition, or you have not trained in a while. During a session,
stop if something hurts. Do not attempt a movement you are not confident you can
do safely, and do not load a bar heavier than you can control just because a plan
said a number.

You train at your own risk. By using Trainr you accept that risk of injury exists
in any physical training, and that you are the one deciding whether a given
session is safe for you on a given day.

## The plans are written by software, and that has limits

The app wrote your plan from the answers you gave. That means:

- It can be wrong. It can suggest an exercise that does not suit you, a weight
  that is too heavy, or a volume that is too much.
- It does not know anything about you that you did not tell it, and it cannot see
  you train.
- Listing an injury makes it try to work around that injury. It is not a
  guarantee that it will.
- Nothing about it is a promise of a result. No plan can promise you strength,
  weight loss, or anything else.

Read your plan before you train it, and change or skip anything that does not
look right for you. You are the last check, not the app.
